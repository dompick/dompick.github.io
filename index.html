7 Majorca facts: all you must know before coming

Essential local knowledge, dialects, climate secrets, and regional highlights before buying your home on the island.

If you haven't been to Majorca or you just landed on the island, this is the ultimate guide with selected Majorca facts to help you navigate your first hours on this territory and avoid getting lost.

Quick Fact Overview:

Mallorca combines 300+ sunny days a year, rich Catalan-Mallorquín linguistic heritage, World Heritage mountains, and a thriving international property market.

Fact 1: The Local Language & Dialect (Mallorquín)

Even though Spanish is the official national language, 6 of Spain’s 17 autonomous communities have co-official languages. In the Balearic Islands, Catalan is widely spoken alongside Spanish. The local variant used in daily life is Mallorquín.

It has very distinctive traits, such as replacing standard masculine and feminine articles (el / la) with se / sa. Rest assured that every Mallorquín-speaking resident also speaks fluent Spanish.

Local words to know:

  • "Mem": Colloquialism roughly translating to "Let's see..."
  • "Ses cames em fan figa": "My legs are turning into figs" (Meaning: I am exhausted from walking!).
  • "Gràcis": Thank you in local dialect.
  • Prefixes "Can", "Son", and "Bini": Frequently used in town and property names (e.g., Son Vida, Can Pastilla). "Son" historically denotes ancestral family property dating back centuries.

Fact 2: Exceptionally Mild Weather & 300 Days of Sun

The climate in Mallorca is enviable all year round. In summer, temperatures range pleasantly from 18°C to 30°C (64°F to 86°F), while winter averages a mild 8°C to 16°C.

Most impressively, Mallorca averages only 52 days of rain per year. Compared to London, Paris, or Berlin, you are almost guaranteed glorious sunny weather during your stay or relocation.

Fact 3: Diverse Coastal & Mountain Regions

The island is geographically divided into distinct zones, each offering unique real estate options:

South-West (Calvià & Andratx)

High-end luxury villas, marinas, vibrant expat communities, and golf courses.

North (Pollença & Alcúdia)

Dramatic bay views, historic old towns, family atmosphere, and tranquility.

Palma & Surrounding Area

Dynamic city life, historic penthouses, fine dining, and year-round connectivity.

Central & Tramontana

Rustic stone fincas, olive groves, mountain peak views, total peace and privacy.

Fact 4: Island Population & Top Cities

Majorca is the largest of the Balearic Islands, with a permanent population of over 900,000 residents across a landmass of 3,640 km². The capital city, Palma de Mallorca, houses nearly half of the island’s population, making it a bustling Mediterranean hub with world-class medical facilities and international infrastructure.

Fact 5: UNESCO World Heritage Tramontana Mountains

The Serra de Tramontana mountain range stretches along the northwest coast and was awarded UNESCO World Heritage status for its stunning terraced agricultural landscape, dry-stone structures, and dramatic sea cliffs. It offers world-famous cycling routes, hiking trails, and picturesque villages like Valldemossa, Deià, and Fornalutx.

Fact 6: Gastronomic Delights

Mallorcan cuisine goes far beyond seaside seafood. Iconically, you will spot passengers at Palma Airport carrying octagonal cardboard boxes filled with freshly baked Ensaimadas (spiral pastries sprinkled with powdered sugar).

  • Sobrasada: Cured raw sausage spread seasoned with sweet paprika.
  • Coca de Trampó: Traditional flatbread topped with diced bell peppers, onions, and olive oil.
  • Frit Mallorquí: A traditional hearty pan-fry of meat, potatoes, garlic, and fresh peppers.

Fact 7: Hidden Secrets & Royal Heritage

Did you know that The Times once voted Palma de Mallorca as the #1 best city in the world to live in? Furthermore:

  • The Spanish Royal Family traditionally spends their summer holidays in Mallorca at the Marivent Palace.
  • Palma’s Cathedral (La Seu) is one of the world's few Gothic cathedrals built directly overlooking the sea frontage.
  • Over 80 historic watchtowers guard the coastline, originally built in the 16th century to spot pirate fleets.

International schools in Majorca

A complete guide to British, IB, American, German, and French schools for families relocating to Mallorca.

Majorca is one of the most prepared destinations for international families in Europe. Thanks to its established cosmopolitan population, the island boasts an exceptional selection of private international schools offering British, International Baccalaureate (IB), German, French, and Swedish curricula.

Why Move to Mallorca with Children?

Relocating to Mallorca offers children an outdoor lifestyle, multi-lingual fluency, and safety. Most international schools teach primarily in English or German while providing mandatory Spanish and Catalan lessons, enabling children to become effortlessly bi- or tri-lingual.

Legal & Financial Guide

Opening a bank account in Spain for a non-resident

Clear legal guidelines, required documentation, and step-by-step procedures to open your Spanish bank account.

Opening a bank account in Spain might look intimidating at first, but it is actually straightforward when you have the right guidance and paperwork prepared.

Why Do You Need a Spanish Bank Account?

If you plan to buy a house in Mallorca or anywhere in Spain, having a Spanish bank account is essential. You will need it to:

  • Pay the property deposit and issue a bank-guaranteed cheque (cheque bancario) at the notary signing.
  • Set up direct debits for local property taxes (IBI, garbage fees).
  • Pay utility bills (water, electricity, high-speed internet, community fees).
  • Apply for a Spanish mortgage.

Resident vs. Non-Resident Accounts

Spanish banking law establishes a distinction between resident accounts (for individuals spending more than 183 days per year in Spain) and non-resident accounts:

Non-Resident Account

Designed for foreign buyers, holiday home owners, or investors before officially moving. Requires periodic non-residency verification.

Resident Account

For full-time residents with a tax residency certificate or Spanish residency card (TIE). Lower maintenance fees.

Documents Required to Open a Non-Resident Account

To comply with Spanish anti-money laundering (AML) regulations, major banks will request the following documents:

  1. Valid Passport or National ID Card: Must be original and valid.
  2. NIE Number (Número de Identidad de Extranjero): Your legal Spanish tax identification number. Dompick can assist you in obtaining this.
  3. Proof of Income / Employment: Payslips, recent tax return, or audited company accounts demonstrating funds origin.
  4. Proof of Address: Recent utility bill or bank statement from your home country.
  5. Certificate of Non-Residency (Certificado de No Residente): Issued by Spanish police or requested directly by the bank on your behalf for a small processing fee.

Which Spanish Banks Are Best for Foreign Buyers?

In Mallorca, major national banks have dedicated international branches with English, German, and French-speaking staff:

  • CaixaBank (HolaBank): Excellent international service packages tailored specifically for non-resident property owners.
  • Banco Santander: Wide network of branches across Mallorca with English mobile banking app options.
  • Sabadell (KeyAccount): Dedicated multi-lingual advisors for home buyers and foreign investors.
  • BBVA: Seamless digital onboarding and robust online banking capabilities.
